# **Gastrointestinal Problems** **<u>in Eating Disorders</u>** 

Gastrointestinal (GI) problems are one of the most common consequences of an eating disorder.  Individuals with Anorexia Nervosa, Bulimia Nervosa, and other eating disorders may experience a number of GI changes, which are believed to be caused by starvation, malnutrition, and the underuse of the GI tract. The misuse of laxatives can also cause GI problems. Laxatives disrupt normal bowel function and can cause symptoms such as: loss of intestinal muscle tone, bloating, gas, colicky pain, appearance of mucus and blood in the stool, incontinence of faeces, and in severe cases, paralysis of the bowel. ## **Why do people with eating disorders experience gastrointestinal problems?** 

There a number of ways that eating disorders disrupt the digestive system. One way is through chronic restriction which can cause the muscles of the small and large intestine to atrophy, or waste away, due to underuse. This slows the movement of food through the digestive system (known as delayed gastric emptying) and can causes stomach aches, bloating and wind. 

The fat that normally surrounds the intestines (mesenteric fat) is also reduced, which further slows digestion. When the body is in starvation mode, it produces fewer enzymes and hormones needed for digestion, which slows the process even more. Vomiting can also interfere with GI functioning  as the rhythm and process of digestion is interrupted by the act of vomiting. 

## **Treating Gastrointestinal Problems** 

GI problems will improve when food intake and behaviours interfering with digestion are  normalised.  If you are working towards increasing the frequency, quantity, and/or variety of food you are eating, it is normal to expect some GI discomfort in the shortterm.  This does not mean you have eaten too much and is a normal part of the recovery journey for many people.  Some people find it helpful to alleviate this discomfort by using a hot water bottle or engaging in a distracting activity after eating.  It may take some time for the system to recover normal functioning. If you are concerned about GI problems it is important you seek professional medical advice. 

Normal digestion through a healthy intestinal tract can vary a lot and can take anywhere from 8-12 hours up to 23-37 hours. For someone with an eating disorder, it is not possible to predict how long digestion will take because the system is disrupted. 



Electrolyte disturbances (especially changes in calcium, sodium, potassium, and magnesium) can affect how the gut muscles work. When these minerals are out of balance, the muscles of the intestines may not contract properly, which further slows down the movement of food.
