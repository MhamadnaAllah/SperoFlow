Weight gain (including fat storage) is a natural and essential biological process during pregnancy. Some people can find the changes in the number on the scale distressing, so it is important to remember that weight gain is appropriate and necessary. 

At times the number on the scale can increase rapidly. This is due to normal growth fluctuations and hormonal changes. It’s especially important to maintain enough water intake to avoid dehydration and rebound swelling as the body seeks to secure nutritional resources. 

It is important that women who are pregnant (or hope to become pregnant) access support so they can prepare for any challenges they may face. Being open and honest about the support you need is essential and can ensure the safe monitoring of you and your baby. 



Support is also available for women who experience an increase in eating disorder thoughts and behaviours after their baby is born. Postpartum can be a particularly challenging time for women, as people often hold unrealistic expectations about returning to a particular body shape or weight. 

- Seeing a healthcare professional (e.g., dietician, psychologist) with experience in eating disorders 

Importantly, many women with eating disorders cope well with pregnancy, and even reduce or cease their eating disordered behaviours altogether.
