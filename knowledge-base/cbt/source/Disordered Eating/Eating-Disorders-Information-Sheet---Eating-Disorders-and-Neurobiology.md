## **Science and Eating Disorders** 

Eating disorders are serious mental health conditions that can lead to significant health problems. Thanks to new research, we now understand more about how eating disorders affect the brain, how they develop, and how we can better support people as they recover.

Brain ‑ imaging studies show that brains can adapt and change throughout recovery. We can “exercise” and change the brain by learning and practicing new ways of thinking and relating to others. ## **The Gene-Environment Interaction** 

“Epigenetics” is the study of biological mechanisms that cause our underlying genetic predispositions to be “switched on” or “switched off”. In certain environments, especially where there is a lot of stress or weight loss, the risk is higher — and the genes might get “switched on”. As international eating disorders expert Professor Cynthia Bulik explains: “Genes load the gun, environment pulls the trigger”. 

Western culture places a high value on appearance and many people dieting or excessively exercise to change the way they look. For some people, these behaviours result in very few negative consequences. For others (with high genetic risk), these environmental influences can trigger their genes to “switch on”, and result in an eating disorder.
