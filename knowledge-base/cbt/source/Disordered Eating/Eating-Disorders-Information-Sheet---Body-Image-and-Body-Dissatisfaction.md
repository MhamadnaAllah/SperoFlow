## **Body Image** 

Body image is a relationship you have with your body. It is built on the thoughts, feelings, attitudes, and perception we have of our body. It can be influenced both negatively and positively over our lifetime by: 



- The way we look after and talk to ourselves about our bodies 

- How others have treated or spoken about our bodies 

- How we experience others talking about and treating their bodies. 

## **Body Dissatisfaction** 

Research studies on body image show that it is normal for people to experience some negativity towards their appearance. 

Most people check the mirror to see how they look, change their hairstyle from time to time and use makeup or other products to ‘improve’ their appearance. 

However, some people become so dissatisfied with their appearance that it starts to negatively impact their mood, physical wellbeing, finances, and even relationships. 

**Consequences of body dissatisfaction can include:** 

- Emotional changes: increased anxiety, depression, anger, or “feeling fat” 

- Thinking changes: strong negative beliefs, preoccupation with weight, shape, or particular body parts 

- Behaviour changes: isolating, restricting diet, driven exercise, seeking surgery or weight loss medication 

- Body checking: frequent weighing, measuring your body, mirror checking, comparing your body to others 

- Body avoidance: wearing baggy clothes, avoiding body exposure (e.g. swimming, change rooms) 

- High importance being placed on appearance: it may have become the most important aspect of life 

## **Improve your body image** 

**Body image is a spectrum** that changes, and being satisfied with our body 100% of the time is not realistic or achievable. We can learn to be more accepting, supportive, and positive towards our bodies over time. There are many things you can do to improve your body image relationship: 

- **Work with your genetics:** Our bodies are genetically wired to be a particular size and shape. Your body will fight to maintain this and forcing your body to change can come at a tremendous cost. 



- **Explore your body image relationship:** How have your body attitudes & beliefs developed? Have they changed over time? Are you holding a body belief that is not yours? 

- **Identify what you** **_do_ like about your body** : Scan your body for things you like rather than dislike. If this is difficult, start with a body part that is not so disliked and practice paying attention to it. 

- **Focus on function:** Consider each body part for its purpose and function (like “my legs allow me to walk, run, and dance”). Explore what you are grateful to your body for. 

- **Engage in activities that help you feel good in your body:** Go to the beach, play a musical instrument, dance, sing, create art, practise yoga or relaxation, have a bath, walk the dog, visit a friend... 

- **Reduce body checking and avoidance:** These behaviours reinforce negative body image.  Read our handouts on _Body Checking_ and _Body Avoidance_ for tips. 

- **Expand your life:** When undue importance is placed on one aspect of life (e.g. appearance), other areas may be neglected. Think of a new activity to try, or an old interest to return to. What can you do to balance areas of your life? 

- **Evaluate your body beliefs as if you were a scientist or lawyer:** Do you have _factual_ evidence to support your beliefs? Do others agree with your belief or judgement? 

- **Identify “feeling fat”:** “I feel fat” can be a way of expressing feelings that are hard to label, understand, or acknowledge. Try to identify the underlying feeling and explore possible triggers. 

- **Consider what makes other people attractive:** Is it purely their appearance? Could it be their personality, attitude, strengths, or actions instead? 

- **Be kind:** Change the way you speak about your body and yourself. Speak to yourself kindly, this matters just as much as your actions.
