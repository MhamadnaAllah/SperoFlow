## **and Eating Disorders** 

### **What is a Vegetarian/Vegan Diet?** 

ensuring adequate intake from non-meat alternatives requires extra thought and planning. 

A vegetarian diet involves abstaining from the consumption of meat (e.g., red meat, poultry, and seafood) and prioritising foods that come from plants. There are a number of variations of this way of eating - some vegetarians will choose to eat fish, while others follow a vegan diet, and choose not to eat any animal products including eggs, dairy products, honey etc. 

Vegan diets often require eating larger volumes of food, which can lead to early fullness or discomfort after meals. High fibre intake may also affect digestion and nutrient absorption.

### **Vegetarian/Vegan Diet and Eating Disorders** 

Following a vegetarian/vegan diet may contribute to restrictive or controlled eating. Some people find they have a strong aversion to eating meat or animal products and feel unable to include it in their diet. If this sounds familiar, it’s important to reflect on your reasons for eating this way. It is also important to assess whether this way of eating began before the onset of your eating disorder, as this can affect your treatment. Ask yourself:

Whole grain bread, potatoes, pasta, and corn contribute small amounts of protein. Since plant-based proteins are typically incomplete, it's important to eat a variety of these foods each day to ensure you meet your full protein requirements.

### **Vitamin B12** 

Vitamin B12 is essential for nerve function and the production of red blood cells, but it's found almost exclusively in animal-derived foods. Sources of B12 include dairy products (for vegetarians), and fortified foods like soy milk and cereals (for vegans). See our handout on the _<u>REAL Food Pyramid</u>_ for more information. 

### **Dietary Needs** 

If continuing with a vegetarian/vegan diet is considered safe by your treatment team, it is essential to work with an experienced dietician to ensure they are getting a healthy balance of nutrients. You will need to ensure adequate intake of protein, vitamin B12, calcium, and iron, as well as less commonly considered nutrients like iodine, selenium, and vitamin D.
