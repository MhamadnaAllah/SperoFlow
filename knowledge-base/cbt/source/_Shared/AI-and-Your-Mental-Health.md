## **When is AI helpful for mental health su ort?** **<u>pp</u>** 

Remember that AI cannot think. This means that it cannot replace human care.  There are some things that AI should not be used for – for your own safety. 

AI can be a helpful tool in supporting your mental health and wellbeing goals — as long as it’s used safely and thoughtfully. Here are some ways you might use it: 

   - AI should not be used in place of real therapy (such as with a counsellor or psychologist) 

   - You should not use AI for personal advice, diagnoses, or treatment plans 

- Practical problem solving: AI can help you see things from different angles and come up with new ideas when you feel stuck (like suggesting ideas for how to prioritise items on your to do list) 

- AI should not be used to make important life choices (like medical, legal, or relationship decisions) 

- Avoid using AI in a crisis or emergency 



- Breaking tasks into smaller steps: Useful if you’re working on graded tasks for depression — starting small and building up 

   - You should avoid using AI for emotional comfort or validation – it can sound empathetic, but it does not understand feelings 

- Exposure planning: You can use AI to help design graded hierarchies for anxiety (like helping you think of ideas for gradual steps between a little and very challenging tasks) 

If you are ever in distress or feel unsafe, it’s important to reach out 

- Motivation and accountability: It can help you plan routines or schedules 

to a real person who can help — such as a trusted friend, your GP, or a crisis service. 

- Writing or journaling support: AI can suggest prompts for reflection, gratitude, or identifying thinking patterns 

## **Tips for safe and helpful use** 

- Simplifying information: It can summarise complex material (like therapy notes or self-help resources) into easier language 

- Use AI to help organise information and support your learning 

AI can also be a way to organise your thoughts, explore ideas, or practice skills between therapy sessions. 

- Avoid sharing personal or identifying information — what you type can be saved and reviewed 

- Consider your wording, be specific in your questions, and prompt AI to give you evidence or sources so you can check the information is correct 

## **When should you use caution with AI?** 

- Check important information with trusted sources (like your psychologist, GP, or reliable websites – see our handout on _Media Literacy_ ) 

AI can be a helpful tool, but it’s important to use it wisely and know its limits. 

- Look out for information that is not accurate. AI can make mistakes and give you ideas that are no longer <u>correct.</u> 

- Keep a curious mindset – not everything AI says will be true or helpful
